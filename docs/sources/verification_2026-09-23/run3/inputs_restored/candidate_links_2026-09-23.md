## Structural claims from the AI (hypotheses to test, not facts)

1. **Shared platforms for communal law:** LexFind/"Tlex", Talus (i-Serve / Nest / Cloud RIO) and,
   in Romandie, "Axsos / iPrime". If any of these hosts many communes' law collections, it is a
   fetcher class and not a one-off source.
2. **Format split:** most mid-sized communes publish PDFs grouped by a systematic index (100/500/700…).
   Larger communes such as Wetzikon, Baar and Köniz would have article-level HTML.
3. **District models:** Schwyz districts are autonomous with their own sites and law. ZH, BE, AG,
   FR, VD and TG host district content inside the cantonal portal. GE, UR, ZG, GL, BS, NW and OW
   have no district tier.
4. **District courts** publish via the cantonal justice portal (e.g. gerichte-zh.ch).
5. **Regional planning associations** (e.g. Planungsgruppe Pfannenstiel, Region Oberaargau) run
   their own sites with Richtpläne and GIS.
6. **Concordats** have no central machine-readable hub. The AI says LexFind indexes the SSIR.
7. **School holidays** are available as .ics files in some cantons.
8. **Pipeline idea from the AI:** a PLZ/commune → Zweckverband bridge table per sector (waste,
   fire, school). This is relevant to jurisdiction mapping (see CONTEXT.md), but only an idea for now.
